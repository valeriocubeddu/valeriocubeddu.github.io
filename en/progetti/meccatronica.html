Servo systems: experimental tests and modelling

Six sessions on real pneumatic, electric and hydraulic test benches. In each one we acquired data in the lab, built a model in MATLAB/Simulink and identified the physical parameters by comparing simulation and measurements.

The method

All sessions follow the same path: bench tests with sinusoidal, square-wave or chirp signals; data acquisition and processing in MATLAB; a model of the system in Simulink; estimation of the parameters that cannot be measured directly, such as friction, damping, resistances and equivalent masses, by minimising the gap between model and measurements with fminsearch or with Simulink Parameter Estimator. The validated model is then used to study the control, the frequency response and the effect of the parameters.

1 · PWM-driven digital valves

Pneumatics

Closed-loop pressure control of a tank with two on/off electro-pneumatic valves, one for filling and one for exhaust. Modulating the duty cycle with PWM gives an average behaviour similar to that of a proportional valve. In the Simulink model we optimised the pipe resistance, the polytropic exponent and the valve conductances, using separate sets of tests for estimation and for validation.

2 · Six-axis force and torque sensor

Measurement

A dynamometer with a Stewart platform structure: six single-axis transducers connected by spherical joints between two plates. We excited it with a mass-spring system, with a natural frequency of about 2.45 Hz, and acquired the signals at sampling rates from 2.5 to 1000 Hz to verify the Nyquist theorem. The physical parameters, i.e. mass, stiffness, lever arm and damping, were obtained by fitting the signals, first with an undamped model and then with damping.

3 · ISWEC on a Stewart platform

Robotics · wave energy

The ISWEC (Inertial Sea Wave Energy Converter) extracts energy from waves using the gyroscopic effect. In the lab the hull motion is reproduced by a six-degree-of-freedom Stewart platform with electromechanical actuators and a cRIO controller. We analysed chirp and sinusoidal tests in heave and pitch, with the platform loaded and unloaded, deriving the Bode diagrams and estimating the translating mass as friction and damping vary.

4 · Electric servo system in position control

Electric

A printer carriage driven by a DC motor through a toothed belt, position-controlled with a PID on a myRIO board and LabVIEW. We acquired responses to sinusoidal set points from 0.5 to 10 Hz and to square waves with different gains. Then we identified, in sequence, the motor resistance and inductance, viscous damping and Coulomb friction, discarding tests with no physical meaning, and compared model and measurements in the frequency domain as well.

5 · Test bench for EMA flight control actuators

Hydraulics · aerospace

A bench developed in the European research project ASTIB (Clean Sky 2) to test electromechanical flight control actuators. A force-controlled hydraulic actuator, supplied at 140 bar, simulates the aerodynamic loads on the position-controlled EMA. We studied the nonlinear model of the bench, with servovalve, bypass orifice, load actuator and control law, compared it with the acquisitions and analysed the effect of servovalve opening, oil temperature and connection stiffnesses.

6 · Hydraulic position control

Hydraulics

A linear hydraulic servo actuator on an upright test rig, used for static and dynamic tests. From the sinusoidal and square-wave tests we studied the effect of the PID gains: increasing Kp reduces the error and widens the bandwidth, but beyond a certain value the system oscillates. We then estimated the moving mass by fitting the inertial force, about 1.2 kg, and verified that the model reproduces the frequency response up to the bandwidth.

What I learned

This lab put me in front of real test benches, with their noise, friction and nonlinearities. I learned that a model is only as good as its validation: choosing different tests to estimate and to verify the parameters, recognising when a result makes no physical sense, and understanding the link between sampling rate, system bandwidth and data quality. Working on different technologies, pneumatic, electric and hydraulic, showed me how the same analysis method applies to very different systems.
