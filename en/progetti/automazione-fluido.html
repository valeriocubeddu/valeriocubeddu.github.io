Pneumatic and electro-pneumatic automation

Eleven lab sessions on industrial fluid automation: from programming a PLC in Ladder to relay logic, from sequential pneumatic circuits to simulation in Simcenter Amesim, up to the experimental characterisation of valves and air bearings.

The sessions

Each session combines circuit design, assembly on the bench and functional verification. Where possible, the measurements were processed in MATLAB and compared with theoretical or standard models. They are listed starting from the closest to industrial automation.

Programming a PLC in Ladder

PLC · Automation

A bench with six pneumatic cylinders, monostable and bistable solenoid valves and reed limit switches, run by a Siemens PLC. From the motion-phase diagram we derived the Grafcet, its condensed version and the auxiliary relay table, then wrote the Ladder program for a semi-automatic cycle, including timers, and tested it on the bench.

Electro-pneumatic systems with relays

Electro-pneumatics

Combinational and sequential logic functions built with relays: OR, AND, memory and pulse limiter. On this basis we designed and wired an electro-pneumatic circuit that drives two cylinders through solenoid valves and limit switches, with the relay diagram derived from the required cycle.

Simulating pneumatic circuits in Simcenter Amesim

Simulation

Two multi-domain models: filling and emptying a tank through a proportional solenoid valve and a restrictor, and speed regulation of a cylinder. We analysed pressures, flow rates, forces and piston speeds as tank volume, restrictor area and viscous friction vary.

Air-lubricated bearings

Measurement · MATLAB

A comparison of aerostatic pads with orifices, grooves and porous surfaces, then bench tests with a load cell, dial gauges and a flow meter. From the data, processed in MATLAB, we derived the static, load, consumption and stiffness characteristics as a function of air gap height, for two supply pressures.

Valve response times and proportional interface

Measurement

With pressure transducers and an oscilloscope we measured the switch-on and switch-off delays of a bistable 4/2 valve. In the second part we characterised a proportional electro-pneumatic interface, i.e. the output pressure as a function of the command voltage, and evaluated the error against the theoretical characteristic by adjusting the zero and span screws.

Pneumatic circuits with sequencers

Pneumatics

Automatic two-cylinder cycles driven by pneumatic sequencer modules, with lever limit switches and threshold-based virtual NOT sensors. The cycle includes single-cycle and continuous modes and the start conditions.

Basic controls and pneumatic circuits

Pneumatics

From elementary controls, OR and AND selectors, automatic return, timed return and sequential control, up to a circuit with two double-acting cylinders that reproduces a real cycle, built from the motion-phase diagram.

Flow rate of pneumatic valves

Measurement · Standards

Flow curves of a valve, i.e. the flow rate as a function of downstream pressure at a fixed upstream pressure, measured on a bench with a calibrated orifice. From the data we computed the conductance and critical pressure ratio according to ISO 6358 and the valve coefficient according to N.B.S., comparing the experimental curves with those from the standard.

Pneumatic cylinders

Pneumatics

Measurement of the breakaway forces of single- and double-acting cylinder pistons, and speed regulation with one-way flow regulators and quick exhaust valves in different configurations.

Pneumatic valves

Pneumatics

Actuation of monostable and bistable valves with positive and negative signals, minimum pilot pressure, a circuit that makes a monostable valve bistable, a pulse limiter and the actuation force of a limit switch.

Bistable fluidic element, ejectors and suction cups

Pneumatics · Vacuum

Supply characteristic of a bistable fluidic element based on the wall-attachment effect, the experimental curve of a vacuum ejector, and pull-off force tests on rigid and bellows suction cups, used in industrial handling.

What I learned

This lab took me from the diagram to a working machine: reading and drawing pneumatic and electro-pneumatic circuits, wiring them on the bench, translating a cycle into Grafcet and then into a Ladder program for a PLC. At the same time I learned to characterise components with measurements and standards, and to use simulation to predict how the circuit changes as the parameters vary.
