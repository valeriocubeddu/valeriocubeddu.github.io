Mechanics of Automatic Machines · Politecnico di Torino · 2024 – 2025

Mechanisms, cams and automatic machines

Ten sessions on the mechanisms that drive automatic machines: six on kinematic and dynamic modelling in MATLAB and Simulink, four on laboratory test benches, where the theoretical models were compared with measurements.

The sessions

Six modelling sessions in MATLAB and Simulink and four tests on real benches, with sensor calibration, signal filtering and comparison with the theoretical model. They are listed starting from the most complex.

Synthesis of a cam system

Simulation

From the required motion to the shape of the cam: a comparison of constant-acceleration, cycloidal, double-harmonic and 3-4-5 polynomial laws, then a program for the analytical synthesis of the profile of a planar cam with a roller follower, checking pressure angle and radius of curvature to avoid undercutting.

Cam transmission

Test bench

A bench with a cam, a preloaded spring follower, a flywheel and position sensors. We estimated the manufacturing error of the cam, on the order of a tenth of a millimetre, and studied the effect of rotational speed and of the flywheel. We then analysed the contact forces to predict the separation between cam and follower, visible in the higher-speed tests.

Gearmotor, indexer and rotary table

Simulation

A Simulink model of a chain made of induction motor, flywheel, gearbox, indexer and rotary table for intermittent rotary motion. Cycloidal and double-harmonic indexer laws are compared, and the effect of flywheel inertia and gear ratio on the table motion is studied.

Rear suspension of a mountain bike

Simulation

The rear end of a full-suspension bike modelled as a four-bar linkage with an air shock. The kinematic analysis gives the relationship between wheel travel and shock compression; in Simulink the dynamic step and frequency responses are studied as damping, rider mass and natural frequency vary.

Follower vibrations

Simulation

The follower modelled with lumped parameters: mass, spring and two dampers, internal and towards the guide. In Simulink, the response to an acceleration step is studied as stiffness and damping vary, together with a comparison of different shapes of the acceleration law and the frequency response.

Slider-crank on a test bench

Test bench

A bench with a DC motor, an encoder and a linear transducer. After calibrating the sensors we derived the lift, velocity and acceleration of the slider, filtered the raw data and compared nine tests, with different offsets, crank radii and speeds, against the theoretical model.

Slotted link and four-bar linkage

Test bench

A four-bar linkage driven by an oscillating slotted link, with three follower-link lengths. From the encoder measurements we derived position, velocity and acceleration of the follower link and compared them with the kinematic model written from the geometry of the bench.

Motion diagrams of a cam system

Simulation

Design of the motion law of a cam-follower system with an asymmetric velocity profile and a modified trapezoidal acceleration: dimensionless lift, velocity and acceleration diagrams as a function of cam angle and of time.

Slider-crank

Simulation

Kinematics of an offset slider-crank with the crank at constant speed: position, velocity and acceleration of the slider in dimensional and dimensionless form, with a parametric analysis on offset, angular velocity and connecting-rod length, and an animation of the mechanism.

Historical mechanisms

Test bench

Late-19th-century teaching models that generate approximate straight-line trajectories. For each one we took the geometric measurements, computed the degrees of freedom, rebuilt the mechanism in the GIM software and carried out kinematic and parametric analyses of the trajectories.

What I learned

This course gave me the tools to design the motion of an automatic machine starting from the specifications: choosing the motion law, deriving the cam geometry, checking pressure angles and vibrations. The lab tests taught me that between model and reality there are always manufacturing errors, sensor noise and phenomena such as follower separation, and that careful calibration and filtering are needed to compare data with theory correctly.
